Why Finland

Course duration

How long are programmes?

Master’s degrees

A university master’s degree is typically 120 ECTS credits, or about two years of full-time study after a bachelor’s degree. Some fields take longer.

Bachelor’s degrees

A university bachelor’s degree is typically 180 ECTS credits, or about three years. UAS programme structures can differ, so confirm the course listing.

Source: Studyinfo, maintained by the Finnish National Agency for Education.

Admissions & eligibility

Check the programme’s exact entry criteria.

Bachelor’s entry

  • Upper-secondary qualification that gives access to higher education in the applicant’s home country.
  • Programme-specific entrance exam, SAT or other assessment may apply.
  • Language evidence depends on the programme and any applicable exemption.

Master’s entry

  • A relevant bachelor’s degree is generally required.
  • UAS master’s programmes also require two years of relevant work experience.
  • Subject prerequisites, portfolio, interview, GMAT or language test may apply depending on the course.

Source: Official admissions guidance. Always check the selected programme in Studyinfo.fi.

Funding your studies

Scholarships are set by individual universities.

Finnish universities may offer competitive scholarships or tuition-fee waivers for eligible non-EU/EEA students. They typically support tuition rather than living costs. Finland’s official study guide warns that there are no general government scholarship programmes for bachelor’s and master’s studies; confirm current terms directly with the university.

Student residence permit

Prepare the application after you receive admission.

Non-EU/EEA students planning degree studies for more than 90 days usually need a Finnish residence permit for studies. Apply through the Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) and follow its checklist for your individual case.

Common application items

  • Valid passport and required identity documents
  • Admission / study-place confirmation
  • Proof of funds: currently at least €800 per month or €9,600 for a year, plus tuition funds if unpaid
  • Valid private health insurance meeting Migri’s requirements
  • Translations, legalisation, photo and other documents requested for your case

After graduation

Eligible graduates can apply for a residence permit to look for work or start a business for up to two years, subject to Migri’s conditions and sufficient funds.

Migri: permit to look for work →

Immigration rules, evidence thresholds, insurance requirements and fees can change. This is a planning summary, not a visa decision. Confirm the latest requirements with Migri’s study-permit checklist before submitting an application.

Student life · Part-time work

Work rules and pay in Finland.

A study residence permit allows paid work in any field for an average of up to 30 hours per week. Some weeks can exceed that limit if the average stays within Migri’s rule over the year. Course-related internship or thesis work may be treated differently under specified conditions.

Pay note: Finland has no statutory national minimum wage. Applicable collective agreements commonly set sector wage floors; confirm the correct agreement and rate for the job. Work eligibility and terms can change—check your permit conditions before working.

FAQs

Common questions about Finland.

How long does a master’s degree take?

A university master’s is typically 120 ECTS credits, or two years of full-time study after a bachelor’s degree; some fields take longer.

How much can an international student work?

With a study residence permit, paid employment is allowed for an average of up to 30 hours per week. Check Migri’s current guidance and your permit conditions.

Is there a Finland government scholarship for bachelor’s or master’s students?

The official Study in Finland guide says scholarships and tuition waivers are offered by individual universities and there are no general government scholarship programmes for these degrees.

What documents are usually needed for the residence permit?

Common items include a valid passport, admission confirmation, proof of sufficient living funds and valid insurance. Migri’s individual checklist is authoritative.
