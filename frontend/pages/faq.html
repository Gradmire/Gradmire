Course-specific questions

Programme types, requirements and subject-area decisions.

For most full-time MBA programmes — including LBS, Oxford Said, Cambridge Judge, and Warwick — you typically need 3–5 years of post-undergraduate work experience. Some programmes accept exceptional candidates with less, but the average profile is 4–5 years. MiM (Master in Management) programmes are designed for candidates with little or no experience and are a strong alternative if you're coming straight from a Bachelor's.
An MSc CS or MSc Data Science is for students with an undergraduate background in computing or a related subject. A conversion course (often called MSc Computing or Graduate Diploma in Computer Science) is designed for graduates from any discipline — you don't need a CS background. Conversion courses are 1 year (taught) and cover fundamentals to bring you up to standard. If you're a non-CS graduate wanting to enter tech, a conversion course is often the most practical path.
Academic Technology Approval Scheme (ATAS) clearance is required for some advanced computing, AI/ML, aerospace, nuclear engineering, and certain materials science programmes at the postgraduate level. It's issued by the UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office and can take 4–6 weeks. Your university will tell you if your course code requires it — and you cannot submit a Student visa application without it if it's required. We flag this for affected students early in the process.
Not necessarily. Russell Group membership signals research intensity and prestige, but it's a blunt instrument. Some specialist universities and post-92 institutions are stronger in specific fields than Russell Group members. For example, the University of Surrey has an excellent computer science department; Aston University's pharmacy and business programmes outperform some Russell Group equivalents in employment outcomes. We always look at department-level reputation, not just institution-level branding.
Triple Crown means a business school holds all three major accreditations: AACSB (USA), AMBA (UK), and EQUIS (Europe). Only about 1% of business schools worldwide hold all three. In the UK, London Business School, Warwick Business School, and a handful of others hold Triple Crown status. It's a strong quality signal for MBA programmes specifically — but many excellent specialist MSc programmes don't need it.

Visa & immigration

The UK Student visa, Graduate Route, and what happens after graduation.

The Graduate Route (previously called Post-Study Work or PSW) lets you live and work in the UK for 2 years after completing a UK degree — or 3 years for PhD graduates. You don't need a job offer to apply, and you can work in any sector at any skill level. You can apply from within the UK up to 6 months before your course ends. From the Graduate Route, you can switch into a Skilled Worker visa if you find a sponsored role.
Standard processing from India takes approximately 3 weeks (15 working days). There's a Priority service available for an additional fee (~£500) that reduces this to around 5 working days. We recommend applying at least 6–8 weeks before your intended travel date to give yourself buffer time. Note: you can apply up to 6 months before your course start date, but not more than 3 months before your CAS is issued.
Yes. On a UK Student visa, you can work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during official vacations and holidays. The exact terms depend on whether your institution is a Highly Trusted Sponsor — almost all established universities are. You cannot work as a self-employed person or set up a business on a Student visa.
You must show enough funds to cover your first year's tuition fees plus: £1,334 per month for up to 9 months if studying in London, or £1,023 per month outside London. These funds must have been held in your bank account for at least 28 consecutive days before you apply. Your CAS number will show the required tuition amount — your living-cost requirement is calculated from that figure.
You can apply for Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR) after 5 continuous years of lawful residence in the UK. Your study years count toward this total if you switch into a qualifying immigration route (like Skilled Worker) after the Graduate Route. The typical path for Indian students: 1–2 years study → 2 years Graduate Route → switch to Skilled Worker → ILR after year 5.
Yes. UK degrees from UK-recognised universities are widely accepted by Indian employers, government departments, and higher education institutions. If you need formal equivalency documentation — for a government job or further study application — the Association of Indian Universities (AIU) provides an equivalency certificate. Fees & scholarships

The real cost of studying in the UK and how to fund it.

Yes, several. The most significant are: Chevening Scholarship (fully-funded, for a one-year Master's, deadline November each year), Gates Cambridge Scholarship (fully-funded, Cambridge only), Commonwealth Scholarships, and university-specific awards from institutions like Edinburgh, Warwick, and Nottingham. Most require early planning — Chevening applications open a year before your course start. We help identify all scholarships relevant to your profile.
Russell Group postgraduate tuition for international students typically ranges from £25,000–£45,000 per year depending on the programme and university. Non-Russell Group universities often charge £14,000–£22,000 for equivalent courses. London-based universities at any tier cost more than those elsewhere. A 1-year UK Master's still costs significantly less in total than a 2-year US or 2-year Australian equivalent, even at the top price point.
All-in for a 1-year UK Master's: tuition (£16k–£45k depending on university and subject) plus living costs. Outside London, you're looking at £22,000–£38,000 total for the year including accommodation, food, and transport. In London, budget £30,000–£55,000. This compares favourably to 2-year programmes in the US or Canada where you'd spend 2–3x as much in total.
As an international (non-UK) student, you are not eligible for the UK government's student loan scheme. Your funding options are: personal/family savings, scholarships and grants, education loans from Indian banks (SBI, Axis, HDFC Credila are common lenders), and a few international lenders like Prodigy Finance and MPOWER that offer unsecured education loans to international students at select universities.
