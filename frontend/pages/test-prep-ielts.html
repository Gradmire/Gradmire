What the IELTS tests

International English Language Testing System

SectionTimeWhat it covers
Listening30 minFour recordings with 40 questions: conversations and talks in everyday and academic settings.
Reading60 minThree long passages with 40 questions (Academic uses texts from books, journals and newspapers).
Writing60 minTask 1: describe a chart, table or process (150 words). Task 2: an essay (250 words).
Speaking11–14 minA face-to-face interview with an examiner, in three parts.
Scores

Scoring and who accepts it

How it’s scored

Each skill gets a band from 0 to 9; the overall band is the average. Universities usually set an overall band and a minimum per skill, for example 6.5 overall with no band below 6.0.

Who accepts it

Universities and immigration authorities in the UK, Australia, Canada, New Zealand and Ireland, plus most US universities. Some UK visas need the separate “IELTS for UKVI” version, so check before you book.

FAQs

IELTS: common questions

Academic or General Training?

Choose IELTS Academic for university study. General Training is mainly for work and migration.

How soon do I get results?

Computer-delivered results usually arrive within a few days; paper-based results take about 13 days. Check the current timing when you book.

Can I retake just one skill?

In some locations IELTS offers a One Skill Retake for computer-delivered tests. Check whether it is available at your test centre and accepted by your universities.

Where do I book the test?

Book directly with the test provider at ielts.org. We help you pick a date that fits your application deadlines.
