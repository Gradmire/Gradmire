Why Kazakhstan

What makes Kazakhstan worth considering

National universities

Leading state medical universities with modern simulation centres.

Stable environment

Kazakhstan is the region’s largest economy, and its cities are well connected.

Moderate costs

Fees are lower than in Europe, with affordable hostels.

Know before you go: Kazakhstan has restructured its medical degrees in recent years. Confirm your programme includes at least 54 months of study plus a 12-month internship at the same university, which licensing bodies such as India’s NMC require.

Eligibility

Who can apply

Typical entry requirements

  • Secondary school completed (Class 12, A-levels, IB or equivalent) with Biology, Chemistry and usually Physics and English.
  • Good science marks. Many universities ask for around 50–60% or more in the science subjects; some set their own entrance exam or interview.
  • Age 17 or older by the start of the course (most universities set 31 December of the admission year).
  • A valid passport and the documents the university and embassy ask for.

Checks that protect your licence

  1. The university is listed in the World Directory of Medical Schools.
  2. The course length and internship meet the rules of the country where you want to practise.
  3. The course is taught in English throughout, if your home regulator requires it.
  4. The degree lets you register to practise in the country where you studied.
  5. You know which licensing exam you will need afterwards (for example USMLE, PLAB/UKMLA, AMC or FMGE/NExT).
Applying from India? NEET and NMC rules
  • NEET-UG qualified. Required to practise in India after an MBBS abroad; the NMC currently accepts the result for three years.
  • PCB marks: at least 50% in Physics, Chemistry and Biology (40% for SC/ST/OBC, 45% for PwD), plus English.
  • The course runs at least 54 months, followed by a 12-month internship at the same university.
  • The whole course is taught in English, and you finish within 10 years of joining.
  • After graduating: pass FMGE (to be replaced by NExT) and complete a 12-month supervised internship in India.

Source: NMC Foreign Medical Graduate Licentiate Regulations, 2021

Student visa

Getting your Kazakhstan student visa

International students apply for a Kazakh student visa on the strength of the university’s invitation.

Typical visa file

  • Invitation from the university (processed through Kazakh migration services)
  • Valid passport and photographs
  • Visa application form
  • Admission letter and fee receipt
  • Medical certificate, including HIV

After you arrive

Register with the migration police after arrival. The university usually arranges this and the annual extensions.

Visa rules depend on your nationality and change often. This is a planning summary, not legal advice. Always follow the current checklist from the embassy and your university. See how we help with visas.

Student life

Living in Kazakhstan as an international student

Climate

Continental: hot summers and very cold winters, especially in Astana.

Food

Major universities have South Asian food options; groceries are affordable.

Language

Russian is widely used in cities; Kazakh is the state language.

After your MBBS

Clinical training and careers after graduation

Practise at home

Most countries ask foreign medical graduates to pass a licensing exam and a period of supervised practice. Check your home regulator’s rules early.

Practise elsewhere

Graduates can prepare for other countries’ licensing routes, such as the USMLE (USA), the UKMLA/PLAB (UK) or the AMC exams (Australia), each with its own eligibility rules.

Postgraduate study

Specialisation options depend on where you are licensed. Plan early if you are aiming for a residency or postgraduate seat.

FAQs

MBBS in Kazakhstan: common questions

Is the course really shorter in Kazakhstan?

Not any more. Choose a programme of about six years in total, including a 12-month internship; shorter programmes may not be accepted by licensing bodies such as India’s NMC.

Are winters a problem?

Winters are harsh in the north. Almaty is milder; factor climate into your choice of city.

Will my MBBS abroad be recognised at home?

Recognition is decided by the medical regulator in the country where you want to practise, not by the university. Most regulators require a listed university, a minimum course length with internship, and a pass in their own licensing exam. Check those rules before you apply.

What is the World Directory of Medical Schools?

It is the global list of medical schools that regulators such as the ECFMG (USA) rely on. Being listed is necessary but not enough on its own: the programme you join must also meet your home regulator’s rules.

Do I have to learn the local language?

Lectures are in English, but clinical training means talking to patients. Most universities teach the local language from year one, and students who take it seriously find hospital rotations much easier.
